## Beginnings

Konica and Minolta had been rivals in cameras and film for decades, but they had also built similar businesses beyond photography. Both had made copiers since the 1960s and 1970s, and by the 2000s both saw office equipment as their core business. In 2000 they began working together on office machines and toner. In August 2003 they merged their management under a new holding company, Konica Minolta Holdings, and in October the group was reorganised into separate business companies.

The merged company brought together 130 years of photographic history: [[m:konica|Konica]]'s roots in an 1873 Tokyo photographic supply shop, and [[m:minolta|Minolta]]'s, in a camera company founded in 1928. It also brought together two logos. The new company kept Minolta's blue globe mark. Konica Minolta describes its five lines as beams of light.

## Growth and turning points

For a short time the new company made cameras and film under the joined name. It sold Konica colour films and Minolta's Dimage digital cameras, and it continued Minolta's autofocus SLR line. In 2004 it launched the Maxxum 7D, a digital SLR that kept Minolta's A mount. Its sensor sat on a moving platform that shifted to cancel camera shake. It was the first interchangeable-lens digital SLR with [[image-stabilisation|stabilisation]] in the body, so every Minolta autofocus lens made since 1985 gained it.

But the market was changing faster than the company. Digital cameras were replacing film, and the silver-halide photographic market was shrinking fast. Konica Minolta later explained that its strengths in optics and mechanics were no longer enough to make competitive cameras on their own, in a business now driven by electronics. In January 2006 it announced it would leave both cameras and film.

## What it made

In its three years in photography, Konica Minolta made Maxxum (Dynax, α) digital SLRs, Dimage compact cameras, colour films, photo paper and minilab equipment. Outside photography it made copiers and printers, which it sold under the bizhub name from 2004, along with medical imaging systems, optical components such as lenses for CD and DVD players, and planetariums.

## Today

Konica Minolta left the camera business in March 2006, after passing part of its digital SLR assets to [[m:sony|Sony]]. Sony took over the A mount and the anti-shake design, and its first digital SLR, the α100 of 2006, came out of them. Konica Minolta withdrew from all photo sales in September 2007.

The company still exists. Renamed Konica Minolta, Inc. in 2013, it makes office printers, production presses, medical imaging, measuring instruments and planetarium projectors. The cameras that Konica and Minolta made for over a century now live on mainly through Sony's α line.
