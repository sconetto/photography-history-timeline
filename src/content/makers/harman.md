## Beginnings

Harman Technology was born from the collapse of [[m:ilford|Ilford]]. As digital cameras took over at the start of the 2000s, demand for film fell fast, and in 2004 Ilford Imaging's British company went into administration. Six of its managers did not want the black-and-white business to die. They bought it, with the factory at Mobberley, in Cheshire, and in February 2005 formed a new company.

They named it after Alfred Hugh Harman, who had founded Ilford in 1879 by making photographic plates in his basement. The new company kept the Ilford Photo brand for its black-and-white films, papers and chemicals, and it still trades under that name. The Mobberley site had been an Ilford paper factory since about 1928, and Ilford's British headquarters since 1983.

## Growth and turning points

The first years were about survival. Harman kept the well-known films such as FP4 Plus, HP5 Plus and Delta in production, and went on making fibre and resin-coated darkroom papers. In 2007 it bought Kentmere, a British maker of photographic paper, and used its name for a lower-priced range of films and papers aimed at students and beginners.

In 2015 Harman was bought by Pemberstone Ventures. By then film was coming back. Young photographers were trying it for the first time, and old cameras were selling again. Harman launched simpler chemistry kits, brought its Ortho Plus film to 35mm and 120 in 2019, and the same year sold a reusable 35mm camera with two rolls of Kentmere Pan 400 for people who wanted to try film without hunting for an old camera.

## What it made

For most of its life Harman made only black-and-white. Colour film is far harder to make, with many more layers and tighter chemistry, and Harman had always said it was out of reach. In late 2022 a small team at Mobberley was given a secret project to find out whether it could be done. On 1 December 2023 Harman launched Phoenix 200, an ISO 200 colour negative film and its first colour film made entirely at Mobberley, from emulsion to cassette. Harman said openly that it was a first attempt and not perfect, with an unmistakably analogue look, and promised that each new colour film would improve on the last. Phoenix came out in 120 rolls in 2024.

## Today

Harman Technology is one of the world's largest makers of film, darkroom paper and photographic chemicals. It sells Ilford and Kentmere black-and-white products and a growing range of Harman colour films, such as the redscale Harman Red 125 of 2025. Its survival is part of the [[film-revival|film revival]]: a business that looked doomed in 2004 now makes new films for a new generation of photographers.
