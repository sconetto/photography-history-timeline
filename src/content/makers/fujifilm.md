## Beginnings

Fuji Photo Film Co., Ltd. was founded in January 1934 under a Japanese government plan to make photographic film at home instead of importing it. It took over the film operations of Dainippon Celluloid, and in February its Ashigara factory started making film, photographic paper and dry plates. The first products were motion-picture positive film and films for printing, followed in 1936 by X-ray film.

Fujifilm soon added optics to its film business. In 1940 it built an optical glass factory in Odawara, and in 1944 it took over an optical company to form Fuji Photo Optical. After the war it began research on colour. Its first colour film, Fuji Color, came out in 1948, and in 1951 it was used for Japan's first colour motion picture. The same year, 1948, it launched its first camera, the Fujica Six, a folding camera for roll film.

## Growth and turning points

Fujifilm grew with Japan's amateur photography boom. Its colour negative films (Fujicolor) and slide films (Fujichrome) competed with [[m:kodak|Kodak]] around the world, and from the 1950s it sold abroad: Southeast Asia first, then a sales company in Brazil and an office in New York in 1958. In 1962 it formed Fuji Xerox with the British company Rank Xerox, which brought Japan its first plain-paper copier.

The 1980s were Fujifilm's most inventive years in photography. In 1986 it released QuickSnap, film with a lens attached, which Fujifilm calls the first one-time-use camera. In 1988 it showed the FUJIX DS-1P, which recorded digital images on a memory card, the start of its digital camera business. The same decade it developed FCR, the first digital X-ray imaging system. As official film supplier to the 1982 World Cup and the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics, it processed photographers' film at the venues and became a global brand.

In 1990 came Fujichrome Velvia, the slide film landscape photographers loved for its vivid colour. In 1996 the Frontier digital minilab let shops print from scans or digital files, and Fujifilm led the move of photo labs to digital. In 1995 Kodak accused Fujifilm of keeping it out of the Japanese market; in 1998 the case before the World Trade Organization went Fujifilm's way.

## What it made

Fujifilm made almost everything in photography: black-and-white, colour negative, slide and cine film; papers and minilabs; [[aps-film|APS]] film and cameras; Fujica SLRs from 1970; and Fujinon lenses for cameras and television. Digital cameras began with the DS-1P and the FinePix line, from 1998.

Its Instax instant system, launched with the Instax Mini 10 in 1998, made credit-card-sized prints. Long after Polaroid's original company had failed, Instax kept selling, and by 2025 Fujifilm had sold more than 100 million Instax cameras and printers.

## Today

Film demand peaked in 2000 and by 2010 had fallen to less than a tenth of that. Fujifilm called its response the "Second Foundation": it looked for new uses of the chemistry and coating skills it had built for film. It moved into medical imaging and drugs, cosmetics, display films, data-storage tape and biotechnology. Kodak filed for bankruptcy in 2012; Fujifilm survived the collapse of film by becoming a much broader company.

It kept photography too. The X100 of 2011 began the X Series, cameras with dials and Fujifilm's film-like colour, and the company moved its cameras upmarket. Its [[g:mirrorless|mirrorless]] X-mount system followed, and in 2017 the GFX system brought a sensor larger than [[g:full-frame|full frame]] to a mirrorless body. Fujifilm still makes Instax film and cameras, some colour film and photographic paper, and the QuickSnap, which passed 1.7 billion sold by its 40th birthday in 2026.
