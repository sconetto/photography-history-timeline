## Beginnings

Apple was founded in California in 1976 by Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak to make personal computers. For its first two decades it had nothing to do with cameras, but its Macintosh computers of the 1980s became the favourite tool of designers and publishers, and [[photoshop|Photoshop]] was first written for the Mac. Many photographers first edited pictures on one.

Apple's first camera came out of its friendship with [[m:kodak|Kodak]]. In 1990 a team of Kodak managers and engineers led by Ken Parulski visited Apple in Cupertino to talk about digital imaging, and in 1992 they came back with a prototype built around a Kodak sensor. Apple designed the body, and on 17 February 1994 it announced the QuickTake 100.

## Growth and turning points

The QuickTake 100 cost $749. It held only eight colour pictures of 640 × 480 pixels, or 32 smaller ones, but it could be plugged straight into a Mac, and it was one of the first digital cameras made for ordinary computer users rather than professionals. The QuickTake 150 followed in 1995. They were not great sellers, and Apple soon left the camera business.

It came back with a phone. On 9 January 2007 Steve Jobs showed the [[iphone|iPhone]], which went on sale in the United States that June. [[camera-phone|Camera phones]] were already common, and the iPhone's 2-megapixel camera was no better than most. What was new was everything around it: a large touchscreen to look at and show pictures, a real web browser, email that displayed photos, and from 2008 the App Store, which let other companies write camera and editing apps. In 2010 Instagram began as an iPhone app. The camera people always carried was now good enough, and pictures could be shared the moment they were taken.

## What it made

With each new iPhone Apple made the camera better, but a phone's lens and [[cmos-sensor|sensor]] are tiny, and they gather little light. So Apple, like other phone makers, turned to [[computational-photography|computational photography]]: the phone takes several pictures and its processor combines them into one better one.

The iPhone 7 Plus of 2016 had two cameras, a wide and a telephoto. A software update that October added Portrait mode, which used both cameras and machine learning to keep the subject sharp and blur the background, the look of a large-sensor camera with a fast lens. The iPhone 11 of 2019 brought Night mode, which made bright, clean pictures in dim light without flash, and Deep Fusion, which used machine learning to bring out texture and detail. In 2020 the iPhone 12 Pro introduced Apple ProRAW, which kept the results of that processing but saved the picture in a [[raw-dng|raw format]], so serious photographers could edit colour and tone as they would a raw file from a large camera.

## Today

The iPhone is one of the most used cameras in the world. Phones like it take the large majority of the world's photographs, and they emptied the market for cheap compact cameras from about 2010. Apple's story shows how much photography changed: its first camera had to be plugged into a computer, and its most important camera is a computer that is always in your pocket.
