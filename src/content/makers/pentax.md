## Beginnings

In 1919 Kumao Kajiwara set up Asahi Optical Joint Stock Co. in Otsuka, Tokyo, to grind lenses for spectacles. "Asahi" means rising sun. The firm soon moved on to binoculars and, in 1923, to the first cinema projection lens made in Japan. By 1931 it had designed a camera lens, which it supplied for the Pearlette, a folding camera made by Rokuoh-sha, the company that became [[m:konica|Konica]].

Asahi Optical became a limited company in 1938 and built a new factory at Ohyama in 1940. After the war, business resumed in 1946 with polishing lenses for binoculars and cameras, and other camera makers bought its lenses. By the early 1950s, the company says, its lens coatings already had a strong reputation.

## Growth and turning points

In 1952 Asahi Optical launched its first camera, the Asahiflex I. It was the first [[g:slr|single-lens reflex]] made in Japan for 35mm film. Its standard lens, the Takumar 50mm f/3.5, gave its name to a long family of Takumar lenses.

Early SLRs had a flaw: the mirror flipped up for the picture and stayed there until the film was wound, leaving the photographer looking at darkness. The Asahiflex IIb of 1954 fixed it with an [[instant-return-mirror|instant-return mirror]] that sprang back as soon as the shutter closed. The 1957 Asahi Pentax added a fixed [[g:pentaprism|pentaprism]], so the viewfinder showed an upright, correctly oriented image at eye level. A sales company named Asahi Pentax had been set up two years before, and the camera's name gradually stood for the whole firm.

Light metering came next. In 1960 Asahi showed a prototype called the Spotmatic at Photokina, an SLR that measured light through its own lens. The production Spotmatic of 1964 used averaging rather than the spot reading its name promised, and it arrived a year after Topcon's RE Super had brought [[ttl-metering|through-the-lens metering]] to market. Still, it was simple and dependable, and it made the Pentax name known around the world. By 1979 the company had made 8 million SLRs. The ES of 1971 went further, with automatic exposure in aperture priority and an electronic shutter. That same year Pentax introduced Super-Multi-Coating, which it describes as the first multi-layer coating for photographic lenses.

In 1975 Pentax replaced its 42 mm screw mount with the K bayonet, which it still uses. The compact MX and ME followed in 1976. The ME-F of 1981, which Pentax calls the first SLR with through-the-lens [[autofocus|autofocus]], showed where cameras were heading, though autofocus only caught on with Minolta's Maxxum 7000 four years later.

## What it made

Pentax built SLRs for every pocket. Beside the 35mm line it made two medium-format systems: the 6×7 of 1969, a 120-film camera shaped like a giant 35mm SLR, and the 645 of 1984. The 645N of 1997 was the first medium-format SLR with interchangeable autofocus lenses. Pentax also made compact cameras by the million, including the Zoom 70 of 1986, one of the first compacts with a zoom lens.

Its lenses kept the company's original strength. Takumars, then SMC Pentax lenses, then the Limited series from 1997, earned a loyal following. From 1977 Pentax also made industrial and medical equipment, including endoscopes, and that business later decided its fate.

## Today

Pentax's first digital SLR, the *ist D, came in 2003, the year after the company renamed itself Pentax Corporation. Pentax put [[image-stabilisation|stabilisation]] in the camera body rather than in each lens, starting with the K100D in 2006, so older K-mount lenses were stabilised too.

But the company was small, and in 2007 [[m:hoya|Hoya]], a maker of optical glass and eyeglass lenses, bought it through a tender offer. The two merged in March 2008. Hoya kept the medical business, which still trades as Pentax Medical, and in October 2011 it sold the camera business to [[m:ricoh|Ricoh]], which set up Pentax Ricoh Imaging. The company has been called Ricoh Imaging since 2013.

Under Ricoh, Pentax has stayed loyal to the digital SLR while most of the industry moved to [[g:mirrorless|mirrorless]]. The K-1 of 2016 brought a [[full-frame-digital|full-frame]] sensor to the K mount for the first time. In 2022, during the [[film-revival|film revival]], Ricoh announced that it would make new Pentax film cameras again. The first, the Pentax 17 of 2024, is a half-frame camera whose lens was modelled on that of the Ricoh Auto Half of 1962.
