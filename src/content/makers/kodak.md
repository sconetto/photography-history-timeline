George Eastman, a bank clerk and keen amateur, started the Eastman Dry Plate Company in Rochester, New York, on 1 January 1881. His goal was to make photography "as convenient as the pencil". He made the business out of roll film, the [[kodak-camera|Kodak camera]] of 1888 and cheap processing, and the company became Eastman Kodak in 1892.

For most of the 20th century Kodak dominated film, paper and chemicals, and invented much of colour photography: [[kodachrome|Kodachrome]], Ektachrome and Kodacolor. Its engineers also built the [[digital-camera-sasson|first digital camera]] (1975) and invented the [[bayer-filter|Bayer filter]] (1976). But a company that made its money on film found it hard to switch to digital, and it filed for bankruptcy in 2012.
