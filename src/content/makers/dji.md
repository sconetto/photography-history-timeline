## Beginnings

DJI began at university. Frank Wang, a student at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, built a small helicopter drone for a school project. In 2006, still a student, he and three friends set up a company to sell it, in a small office in Shenzhen, the electronics city just across the border from Hong Kong.

DJI started by selling helicopter drones and their parts to keen hobbyists. It built its own motors, navigation units and gimbals, the motorised mounts that keep a camera steady. Many of those hobbyists already used drones to take photographs from the air, which until then usually meant hiring a light aircraft or helicopter for thousands of dollars. By 2011, when DJI made its first quadcopters, Wang was the only one of the founders left.

## Growth and turning points

The Phantom of 2013 changed everything. It was DJI's first fully assembled drone and one of the first designed for ordinary consumers. It cost $629, held its position using GPS, and could be flown by most beginners straight out of the box, when other quadcopters were very hard for newcomers to control. It carried a GoPro action camera on a DJI mount, and later Phantoms had their own stabilised cameras. Aerial photography, once a job for professionals with hired aircraft, became a hobby.

DJI moved quickly from there. It made larger drones for film-making, crop-spraying drones from 2015, and, in the same year, the Osmo, a small 4K camera on a handheld gimbal. In 2016 the Mavic Pro folded up to about the size of a water bottle, so a camera drone could go in an ordinary bag. By some estimates DJI held about three-quarters of the market for personal drones.

## What it made

DJI's products are cameras as much as aircraft. What made its drones useful for photography was the gimbal, which holds the camera level and steady while the aircraft tilts and shakes in the wind, so that a small flying machine could take sharp pictures and smooth video. The same idea went into its handheld cameras such as the Osmo, a form of [[image-stabilisation|image stabilisation]] done with motors rather than inside the lens or sensor.

In 2015 DJI bought a stake in the Swedish camera maker [[m:hasselblad|Hasselblad]], and from 2017 it held a majority. The Mavic 2 Pro of 2018 was the first DJI drone with a Hasselblad camera, built around a 20-megapixel 1-inch-type sensor.

## Today

DJI is the world's largest maker of consumer drones and is still based in Shenzhen. Its success has also brought it into conflict with the United States government, which has long questioned whether Chinese-made drones are a security risk; DJI says they are not. In December 2025 the US Federal Communications Commission added foreign-made drones and their key parts to its Covered List, so new DJI models can no longer be approved for sale there, although drones already approved can still be used.
