## Beginnings

Konica's story begins in 1873, earlier than any other Japanese maker on this site, and it starts with a portrait. Rokusaburo Sugiura, from a family of Tokyo medicine wholesalers, had his photograph taken at a studio at the age of 25 and was so struck by it that he decided to sell photographic supplies. In 1873 he began selling lithographic and photographic tools and materials at the family shop, Konishiya Rokubeiten, and he later opened his own shop, Konishi Honten, in Nihonbashi.

Konishi soon started making what it sold. From 1882 it built lightproof boxes for photography and lithographic presses, so Japanese printers no longer depended on imports. In 1897 it imported cinematograph equipment, and a 21-year-old clerk used it to film horse-drawn trams in Nihonbashi, one of the first films shot by a Japanese.

In 1902 it opened the Rokuosha factory in Shinjuku to make photographic materials. In 1903 it launched Sakura photographic paper, the first made in Japan, and the Cherry Hand Camera, Japan's first branded camera made in quantity.

## Growth and turning points

Film came next. In 1929 the company marketed Sakura Film, its own roll film for everyday photography, and in 1933 Sakura X-ray Film, ending Japan's dependence on imported X-ray film. Konishi had brought X-ray equipment to Japan as early as 1896, a year after Röntgen discovered X-rays. In 1936 the business became a joint-stock company, Konishiroku Honten. Sakura Natural Color Film, the first colour film made in Japan, was announced in 1940.

The company also trained photographers. In 1923 it founded the Konishi Professional School of Photography, today the Tokyo Polytechnic University. In 1951, when commercial radio began in Japan, it aired the country's first advertising jingle, "I Am an Amateur Cameraman", which did not mention the company at all.

In the 1970s Konica built cameras that took the work out of photography. Its C35 EF of 1975, nicknamed Pikkari Konica, was the first camera with a built-in flash. Two years later the C35 AF, nicknamed Jaspin Konica, became the first [[autofocus|autofocus]] camera on the market. Konica's research had found that 36% of failed pictures were out of focus. The C35 AF used a Honeywell module that compared two views of the scene and set the lens by itself. Autofocus compacts soon filled the shops.

## What it made

Konica was always a film company as much as a camera maker. In 1976 it introduced the first 24-exposure roll. In 1987 it launched GX3200 Professional, a colour film rated at ISO 3200. The same year the company renamed itself Konica Corporation and put every product under the Konica name.

Its cameras ranged from cheap compacts to the Hexar of the 1990s, a quiet autofocus compact with a fast 35mm lens. Like other photographic firms, Konica found other uses for what it knew. Its film coating skills led to medical X-ray systems, and its U-Bix480 of 1971 was the first plain-paper copier built with Japanese technology. Its lens makers turned to the tiny plastic lenses that read CDs and DVDs, and Konica became the world's top supplier of them.

## Legacy

By the late 1990s digital cameras were cutting into film, Konica's main photographic business. In 2000 Konica and its old rival [[m:minolta|Minolta]] began working together on office equipment, and in August 2003 they merged into [[m:konica-minolta|Konica Minolta]]. Three years later the merged company left photography. Konica film, and the Sakura name that went back to the photographic paper of 1903, left the market. The company still makes medical imaging systems, office equipment and planetarium projectors.
