## Beginnings

Mamiya began with an inventor and an investor. Seiichi Mamiya had designed a new way to focus a folding camera: instead of moving the lens, it moved the film. Tsunejiro Sugawara put up the money. In May 1940 they set up Mamiya Koki Seisakusho in Hongo, Tokyo, and started making cameras.

The first, the Mamiya Six, was a folding camera for 120 roll film that used this back-focusing design. It sold through the war years, and in 1946 the company built a factory in Setagaya to make its own shutters and lenses. In 1950 it became a corporation, Mamiya Koki Co., Ltd., and in 1953 it listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

## Growth and turning points

Mamiya made 35mm cameras, including SLRs, but it found its place in medium format, the larger film sizes professionals used. Its twin-lens reflexes, from the Mamiyaflex C of 1956 to the C330 of 1969, did something almost no other TLR did: their pair of lenses could be swapped for wider or longer ones, and a bellows let them focus close.

In 1970 came the RB67 Professional, a large 6 × 7 cm [[g:slr|SLR]] built for the studio. Its back rotated for vertical or horizontal frames, its bellows focused close without extra tubes, and its film backs could be changed mid-roll. Its improved Pro S version of 1974 then stayed in production for 17 years before its next model change. The smaller M645 of 1975 put medium format in a camera that could be carried all day, and the RZ67 of 1982 brought electronics to the RB67's design.

In 1989 Mamiya revived its first name with the New Mamiya 6, a 6 × 6 cm rangefinder with interchangeable lenses.

## What it made

Mamiya was rarely a household name, and its 35mm cameras never sold well, but for decades many fashion, advertising, portrait and wedding photographers worked with its cameras. It made Sekor lenses for its systems, press cameras for news and commercial work, and, under the Mamiya name, 35mm SLRs and compacts. Its parent company changed many times: Mamiya Koki was absorbed in 1992 into Olympic Co., a maker of fishing tackle and golf shafts, which renamed itself Mamiya-OP in 1993.

## Today

Film's decline hit medium format hard. In September 2006 Mamiya-OP withdrew from the optical business, and the camera line passed to a new company, Mamiya Digital Imaging. It made digital backs and medium-format digital cameras, and in March 2009 the Danish digital-back maker [[m:phase-one|Phase One]] made a large investment in it, taking 45% and leadership of research, marketing and distribution. In December 2015 Phase One bought the business outright, along with Mamiya's camera, shutter and lens design, and set up Phase One Japan.

Mamiya's former camera factories now work for Phase One. Mamiya-OP, the old parent, still exists, making electronics and sports equipment.
