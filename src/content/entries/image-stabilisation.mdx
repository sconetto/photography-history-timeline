## How it began

Shake from the photographer’s hands blurs pictures at slow [[g:shutter-speed|shutter speeds]], especially with long lenses. In 1994 Nikon sold a compact with optical stabilisation, and in 1995 Canon launched the first stabilised SLR lens.

## How it works

Tiny gyroscopes detect how the camera rotates. A processor drives a floating lens group, or the sensor itself, in the opposite direction so the image stays still on the film or sensor during the exposure.

## Why it mattered

Photographers could shoot several [[g:stop|stops]] slower without a tripod, in dim light or with long lenses. It made small, light telephotos and phone night photos practical.

## Limits and what replaced it

It freezes the camera’s movement, not the subject’s. Phones add electronic stabilisation, combining many frames by software.
