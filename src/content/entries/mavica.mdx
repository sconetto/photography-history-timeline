## How it began

In 1981 Sony showed a prototype camera that used no film: the Mavica, for magnetic video camera. It recorded up to 50 pictures on a small magnetic disk.

## How it works

A [[ccd|CCD]] sensor produced a video signal that was written to the disk in analogue form, like a single frame of television. The pictures were viewed on a TV set.

## Why it mattered

It shocked film companies, and Japanese electronics makers began the race to filmless cameras. Still video cameras went on sale later in the decade and were used by some newspapers.

## Limits and what replaced it

Its resolution was only TV quality, and analogue recording could not be edited on computers. Digital cameras replaced it in the 1990s.
