## How it began

In 1987 Thomas Knoll, a PhD student, wrote a program to show greyscale images on his Mac. His brother John, at Industrial Light & Magic, saw what it could do. Adobe licensed it and released Photoshop 1.0 in 1990.

## How it works

A picture is a grid of pixels, and Photoshop changes their values: brightness and contrast with curves, colours, retouching and cloning, and from 1994 layers that can be stacked and masked.

## Why it mattered

It moved the darkroom onto the computer. Magazine production, advertising and then photographers adopted it. It also made changing pictures easy, raising questions about trust in photographs.

## Limits and what replaced it

Lightroom and other tools now handle everyday edits of [[raw-dng|raw files]]. AI editing and [[generative-ai|generative tools]] carry its questions much further.
