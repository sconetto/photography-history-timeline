## How it began

Earlier digital SLRs were Kodak conversions of film cameras and cost $15,000 or more. In 1999 Nikon launched its own design, the D1, at about $5,500.

## How it works

Its [[ccd|CCD]] sensor measured 23.7×15.6 mm, close to the APS film frame, so lenses gave a narrower view than on [[format-135|35mm]]: a crop factor of 1.5. It could shoot four and a half frames per second, fast enough for sport.

## Why it mattered

News agencies switched in large numbers, and within a few years film had almost gone from newsrooms. Its sensor size, which Nikon called DX, became the [[aps-c|APS-C]] standard.

## Limits and what replaced it

Its 2.7 megapixels were enough for newspapers but not for large prints. Its successors, and [[full-frame-digital|full-frame]] cameras, overtook it within a few years.
