## How it began

The [[kodak-camera|Kodak]] of 1888 cost $25, a lot of money. In 1900 Kodak launched the Brownie, designed by Frank Brownell and named after popular cartoon characters, at one dollar. It was advertised to children, but adults bought it in huge numbers.

## How it works

It was a cardboard box with a simple lens, a fixed [[g:aperture|aperture]] and a rotary shutter. The film was loaded in daylight, and the owner pointed, pressed and wound on, then sent the film for processing.

## Why it mattered

Over 150,000 were sold in the first year. The Brownie turned photography into an everyday habit and created the family snapshot. Its successor, the Brownie No. 2 of 1901, introduced the [[film-120|120 film]] still used today.

## Limits and what replaced it

Fixed focus and a single shutter speed meant it needed bright light and still subjects. Folding cameras and, later, the [[instamatic]] offered more for little extra cost.
