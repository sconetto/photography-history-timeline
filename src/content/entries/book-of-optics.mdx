## How it began

Ibn al-Haytham, a scholar from Basra working in Cairo, wrote his seven-volume *Kitāb al-Manāẓir* in the early 11th century. Before him, many thinkers held that the eye sends out rays to touch what it sees.

## How it works

He reasoned and tested the opposite: light comes from objects into the eye. In one experiment he set several lamps outside a dark chamber with a small opening. Each lamp made its own separate spot of light on the inside wall, opposite the lamp. Covering a lamp removed only its spot, which showed that the light travelled in straight lines through the hole without mixing.

## Why it mattered

His careful, repeatable experiments made optics an experimental science. Latin translations of the book shaped European optics for centuries, including the work of Kepler, who explained the camera obscura and the eye.

## Limits and what replaced it

His account of how the eye forms an image was incomplete. Kepler, in 1604, was the first to explain that the eye's lens forms an inverted image on the retina, as a lens does in a camera.
