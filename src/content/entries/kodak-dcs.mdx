## How it began

Kodak, where Steven Sasson had built the [[digital-camera-sasson|first digital camera]], fitted a sensor to the back of a Nikon F3. Launched in 1991, the DCS 100 was aimed at newspapers.

## How it works

The [[ccd|CCD]] sensor replaced the film back. Images went by cable to a Digital Storage Unit carried on the shoulder, which held about 150 pictures and had a small screen. Pictures could be sent by phone line.

## Why it mattered

It showed that digital pictures could reach a newsroom minutes after an event. Agencies bought it, and the DCS line set the pattern of a professional digital SLR built on a film body.

## Limits and what replaced it

It was costly, heavy and low in resolution. Self-contained cameras like the [[nikon-d1|Nikon D1]] made it obsolete by 1999.
