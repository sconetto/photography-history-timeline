## How it began

Most digital SLRs used film-era lens mounts. Olympus and Kodak proposed a standard designed for digital sensors: Four Thirds, launched with the Olympus E-1 in 2003. Olympus and Panasonic followed in 2008 with Micro Four Thirds, the same sensor without a mirror.

## How it works

The sensor is 17.3×13 mm, with a 4:3 shape like a computer screen. Its diagonal is half that of [[format-135|35mm]], so the crop factor is 2. Lenses were designed to send light straight at the sensor.

## Why it mattered

It was an open standard, so lenses from different makers fit. Micro Four Thirds made the first mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera, the [[lumix-g1|Lumix G1]], and small, light systems.

## Limits and what replaced it

The smaller sensor gives more [[g:noise|noise]] and less control of [[g:depth-of-field|depth of field]] than APS-C or full frame. Four Thirds SLRs ended in 2010; Micro Four Thirds continues.
