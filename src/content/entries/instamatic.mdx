## How it began

Many people found threading 35mm film fiddly and spoiled rolls. In 1963 Kodak launched the 126 cartridge and the Instamatic camera to use it, in the tradition of the [[brownie|Brownie]].

## How it works

The film runs from one side of a plastic cartridge to the other, so it never needs rewinding. The cartridge only fits one way. A notch tells better models the film speed, and the flash could be a cube of four bulbs that rotated after each shot.

## Why it mattered

It was one of the best-selling camera lines ever and made colour snapshots the norm. Its cartridge idea led to the smaller [[film-110|110]] in 1972.

## Limits and what replaced it

The film did not lie perfectly flat, and frame registration was loose, so pictures were soft. Small autofocus 35mm compacts replaced it in the 1980s.
