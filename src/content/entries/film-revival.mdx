## How it began

Film sales collapsed in the 2000s, and many products disappeared: Polaroid stopped instant film in 2008 and Kodachrome ended in 2009. Small companies such as the [[m:impossible|Impossible Project]] and Lomography, and the rescued [[m:ilford|Ilford]] business, kept making film for a dedicated audience, which began to grow in the 2010s.

## How it works

The chemistry is unchanged, but the economics are new: smaller batches, reopened coating lines and second-hand cameras. Many young photographers shoot film for its [[g:grain|grain]] and colour, for the slower way of working, and for having a negative.

## Why it mattered

It showed that an older technology could survive alongside its replacement for reasons of taste rather than performance. Kodak brought back Ektachrome, Harman launched a new colour film, and in 2024 Ricoh released the Pentax 17, its first new film camera in over twenty years.

## Limits and what replaced it

Film and processing are now expensive and labs are scarce. Most people still photograph with phones; film is a choice, not the norm.
