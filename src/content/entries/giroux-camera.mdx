## How it began

When the French state published the [[daguerreotype]] in August 1839, Daguerre had already arranged for Alphonse Giroux, a Paris dealer related to him by marriage, to make and sell the equipment. Each camera carried a plate with Daguerre’s signature to show it was genuine.

## How it works

Two wooden boxes slide inside each other to focus. A Chevalier [[achromatic-lens|achromatic lens]] at the front forms the image on a ground-glass screen; once focused, the screen is replaced by the sensitised silver plate. A brass cover over the lens served as the shutter.

## Why it mattered

It made photography something you could buy. The kit, with camera, plates, iodine box and mercury box, cost about 400 francs, and its design was copied widely in the first years of photography.

## Limits and what replaced it

It was big, heavy and slow; the lens could not take portraits in reasonable time. Smaller metal cameras with the faster [[petzval-lens|Petzval lens]] replaced it within two years.
