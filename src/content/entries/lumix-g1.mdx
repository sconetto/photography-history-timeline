## How it began

Compact digital cameras showed a live image from the sensor, but serious cameras kept the SLR mirror. Panasonic’s G1 of 2008, the first [[four-thirds|Micro Four Thirds]] camera, dropped it.

## How it works

Light goes straight to the sensor, which feeds an electronic viewfinder and the rear screen. Without a mirror box the lens can sit close to the sensor, so bodies and wide lenses can be smaller.

## Why it mattered

It started the mirrorless camera, which shows the exposure before the shot and focuses using the sensor itself. After the [[sony-a7|Sony α7]] in 2013, mirrorless cameras replaced SLRs.

## Limits and what replaced it

Early electronic viewfinders lagged and drained batteries, and contrast-detect focusing was slow for action. Better screens and on-sensor phase-detect focus fixed both.
