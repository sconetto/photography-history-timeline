## How it began

German 35mm cameras such as the Leica and Contax were expensive. Argus, a company in Ann Arbor founded to make radios, set out to make an affordable American 35mm camera and launched the C3 in 1939.

## How it works

The C3 is a box with a lens, a coupled rangefinder linked to the focus by a gear, and a separate viewfinder. Its odd, blocky shape came from building it like a radio, around a simple chassis.

## Why it mattered

It sold for about $30 and was tough and reliable. For more than two decades it was the 35mm camera most Americans could afford, and it made colour slides on [[kodachrome|Kodachrome]] a mass hobby.

## Limits and what replaced it

It was heavy, slow to use and its finder was separate from the rangefinder. Japanese rangefinders and SLRs, cheaper and better, ended its run in the 1960s.
