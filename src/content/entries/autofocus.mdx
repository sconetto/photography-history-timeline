## How it began

Focusing by hand was slow and easy to get wrong. In 1977 Konica's C35 AF used a Honeywell module that compared two views of the scene, like an automatic [[g:rangefinder|rangefinder]]. In 1985 Minolta's Maxxum 7000 brought fast autofocus to the [[g:slr|SLR]].

## How it works

Phase detection splits light from opposite sides of the lens and measures how far the two images are shifted, which tells the camera both which way and how far to move the focus. Contrast detection moves the lens until fine detail is sharpest.

## Why it mattered

It made sharp pictures of moving subjects routine and helped cameras sell to people who had never focused one.

## Limits and what replaced it

Early systems hunted in low light and on plain surfaces. Modern cameras put phase-detection pixels on the sensor itself and use subject recognition to decide what to focus on.
