## How it began

Hércules Florence was a French-born painter who settled in Campinas, in the province of São Paulo, after a scientific expedition through the Brazilian interior. With no printing presses nearby, he looked for a way to reproduce his drawings and labels. From around 1833 he experimented with paper sensitised with silver salts, both in a camera obscura and by contact printing.

## How it works

Like Schulze before him, Florence relied on silver salts that darken in light. He printed designs drawn on glass onto sensitised paper by sunlight, and stabilised the results well enough to use them as labels and diplomas. His notebooks call the method *photographie*: drawing with light.

## Why it mattered

Florence shows that photography was being invented in several places at once. His use of the word *photographie* predates John Herschel's "photography" of 1839. Because he worked far from Europe's scientific societies, his priority was not recognised until the Brazilian historian Boris Kossoy studied his papers in the 1970s.

## Limits and what replaced it

His process was little known and made no lasting camera images. The methods that spread around the world came from Daguerre and Talbot in 1839.
