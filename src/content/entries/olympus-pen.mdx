## How it began

Olympus asked the young engineer Yoshihisa Maitani to design a camera that would sell for ¥6,000, much less than other cameras. He chose the half frame: the original 18×24 mm cine frame, half of [[format-135|35mm]].

## How it works

The frame runs vertically along the film, so the camera is held upright for landscape-shaped pictures. Halving the frame doubles the number of shots per roll and allows a smaller lens and body.

## Why it mattered

It was a hit, and by the mid-1960s half-frame cameras were a large part of the Japanese market. Maitani went on to design the OM series and the XA, and the Pen name returned in 2009 on Micro [[four-thirds|Four Thirds]] cameras.

## Limits and what replaced it

The small negative gave less detail, and labs often printed half-frames badly. The boom faded by the late 1960s, as full-frame compacts got smaller.
