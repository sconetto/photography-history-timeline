## How it began

Photographers moving to digital SLRs found their wide-angle lenses no longer looked wide on smaller [[aps-c|APS-C]] sensors. In 2002 the Contax N Digital and Canon's EOS-1Ds offered sensors the full size of the [[format-135|35mm frame]]. Canon's EOS 5D (2005) brought the price within reach of many more photographers.

## How it works

A 36 × 24 mm sensor captures the whole image that a 35mm lens projects, so there is no [[g:crop-factor|crop factor]]: a 24mm lens is as wide as it was on film. The larger area collects more light at a given exposure, which means less noise, and gives shallower depth of field at the same framing and f-number. See [[p:formats|formats and crop factor]].

## Why it mattered

Full frame became the standard for professional and enthusiast cameras, and the term "full frame" is itself a digital-era name for Barnack's frame. Since 2013 it has been the heart of mirrorless systems from Sony, Canon, Nikon, Panasonic and Leica.

## Limits and what replaced it

Full-frame sensors and lenses are bigger, heavier and costlier. Digital medium format (about 44 × 33 mm) goes larger still; for many people, phones and smaller formats are enough.
