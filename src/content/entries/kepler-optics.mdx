## How it began

Kepler came to optics through astronomy: he wanted to know why pinhole images of the Sun during an eclipse looked the size they did. In 1604 he published a book of optics that built on Ibn al-Haytham and the medieval writer Witelo, and used the name camera obscura, "dark chamber", for the device.

## How it works

Kepler showed that the eye’s lens throws an inverted image onto the retina, exactly as a lens throws one onto the back wall of a camera obscura. In his 1611 Dioptrice he traced rays through convex and concave lenses and explained how a telescope magnifies.

## Why it mattered

He made the camera a model of vision and laid the ground for designing lenses by geometry rather than trial. Portable camera obscuras, including tents and boxes, followed.

## Limits and what replaced it

Kepler did not have the exact law of refraction. Snell’s law, found a few years later, made precise lens calculations possible.
