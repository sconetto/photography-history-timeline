## How it began

Websites for storing photos existed by the late 1990s. Flickr, launched in Vancouver in 2004, added tags, comments, groups and Creative Commons licences, and became the meeting place for photographers online.

## How it works

Photos are uploaded and resized for the web as [[jpeg|JPEGs]]. Others can comment, favourite and follow. Instagram, launched in 2010 for the [[iphone|iPhone]], made sharing a single step from the camera.

## Why it mattered

Photography became a way of talking, and pictures of the moment replaced the family album. Flickr’s Creative Commons pool also became a large source of freely licensed photographs.

## Limits and what replaced it

Shared pictures are compressed and often fleeting. Platforms rise and fall, and their rules on privacy, ownership and algorithms shape what gets seen.
