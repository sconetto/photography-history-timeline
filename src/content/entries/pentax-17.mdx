## How it began

By the 2010s the big camera makers had stopped designing film cameras. Nikon's last new film camera, the F6, came in 2004, and Canon's last new film compact in 2005. [[m:leica|Leica]] kept making its costly mechanical M cameras, but almost every other new film camera was a simple plastic point-and-shoot or a reusable copy of a disposable. Meanwhile the [[film-revival|film revival]] was bringing young photographers to film, often with cameras older than themselves.

In December 2022 [[m:ricoh|Ricoh Imaging]] announced the Pentax Film Camera Project, a plan to design new [[m:pentax|Pentax]] film cameras. Few of its engineers had ever built one, so it paired experienced designers, some of them called back from retirement, with younger staff, and restarted the making of parts that had long been out of production. The first result, the Pentax 17, was announced on 18 June 2024.

## How it works

It is a [[olympus-pen|half-frame]] camera: each picture is 17 × 24 mm, half of a [[format-135|35mm]] frame, so a 36-exposure roll gives 72 pictures. The film runs across the body as usual, so when the camera is held normally it takes upright pictures, the shape of a [[camera-phone|phone]] photo. The 25 mm f/3.5 lens, like a 37 mm lens on [[g:full-frame|full frame]], is a three-element design based on the lens of the 1994 Pentax Espio Mini. Ricoh chose its [[g:angle-of-view|angle of view]] with the 1962 Ricoh Auto Half in mind.

Instead of focusing precisely, the user picks one of six distance zones, from 25 cm to infinity, marked with symbols. A meter sets the [[g:exposure|exposure]] in one of seven modes, including flash and bulb, with a [[g:leaf-shutter|leaf shutter]] running from 1/350 to 4 seconds. The film is wound on with a lever and rewound with a crank, both by hand: Ricoh left these steps manual on purpose, for the feel of a film camera.

## Why it mattered

It was the first new 35mm film camera designed by a major camera maker in nearly twenty years, leaving aside Leica's M cameras and reissues such as Leica's new M6 of 2022. It was neither a reissue nor a toy: it had a newly designed lens, magnesium-alloy top and bottom covers and a price of $499.95, between the cheap plastic cameras and the expensive ones.

Demand was far greater than Ricoh expected. Two days after the announcement it stopped taking orders on its own shops in Japan and warned that deliveries would take a long time. The camera showed that a large company could still design and build a film camera, and that a new generation of film photographers would pay for one.

## Limits and what replaced it

It is a simple camera. Zone focusing takes practice, and in dim light the f/3.5 lens needs slow shutter speeds, the flash or a tripod. The half frame holds less detail than a full 35mm frame. Some reviewers found the body plasticky and the camera too simple for its price, which is more than many good second-hand film cameras cost. Film and processing are expensive too. It replaced nothing: it is a choice made alongside digital and phone cameras.
