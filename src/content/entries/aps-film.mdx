## How it began

In 1996 five companies, Kodak, Fujifilm, Canon, Minolta and Nikon, launched the Advanced Photo System to win back amateurs with easier, smaller cameras. Kodak sold it as Advantix.

## How it works

The film was 24mm wide, in a cartridge that the camera loaded and rewound by itself. A transparent magnetic layer recorded settings and the chosen print shape. Every frame was exposed at 30.2 × 16.7 mm, and the lab printed it as H (wide), C (classic 3:2, cropped to 25.1 × 16.7 mm) or P (panoramic).

## Why it mattered

APS itself was a commercial disappointment, overtaken by digital within a few years. But early digital SLR sensors were about the size of its C frame, so the industry called them [[aps-c|APS-C]], a name that lasted long after the film. See [[p:formats|formats and crop factor]].

## Limits and what replaced it

Its negatives were about 40% smaller than 35mm frames, so quality suffered in big enlargements, and digital compacts soon outdid it on convenience.
