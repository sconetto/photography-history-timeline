## How it began

Variable focal-length lenses were used for film and television cameras from the 1930s. In 1959 Frank Back of Zoomar designed, and Voigtländer made, a 36–82mm [[g:zoom-lens|zoom]] for 35mm single-lens reflexes, the first for still photography.

## How it works

Groups of elements slide along the lens. One group changes the magnification, and another moves to compensate so the image stays in focus, so the photographer can frame by zooming instead of walking or changing lenses.

## Why it mattered

Early zooms were heavy and less sharp than fixed lenses, but computer-aided design and better [[lens-coating|coatings]] made them good enough for nearly everyone by the 1980s. A single zoom replaced a bag of lenses for most photographers.

## Limits and what replaced it

Zooms are bigger, slower or more complex than prime lenses of similar quality. Phones combine several fixed-focal-length cameras with digital cropping instead.
