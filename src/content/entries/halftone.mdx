## How it began

For forty years photographs reached print only by being copied into wood engravings by hand. On 4 March 1880 the New York *Daily Graphic* printed a photograph of a shantytown directly, using a crude screen. Through the 1880s Frederic Ives and others refined the method, and ruled glass screens made it practical.

## How it works

A press prints ink or no ink; it cannot print grey. The photograph is copied through a fine screen that turns each area into dots: big dots where it is dark, small ones where it is light. From reading distance, the eye sees continuous tones.

## Why it mattered

It made photographs part of the printed page, and with that came picture magazines, photojournalism and advertising photography.

## Limits and what replaced it

Coarse screens lose fine detail and newsprint makes dots spread. Nothing replaced the principle; digital screening and inkjet dithering are refinements of it.
