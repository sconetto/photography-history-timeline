## How it began

Kodak’s [[instamatic|Instamatic]] cartridge of 1963 made loading easy, but the cameras were still bulky. In 1972 Kodak shrank the idea: 110 film and the Pocket Instamatic, flat enough to carry in a pocket.

## How it works

Like 126, the film runs between two chambers of a plastic cartridge that drops into the camera. The frame is only 13×17 mm, about a quarter of 35mm, and a tab on the cartridge tells the camera the film speed.

## Why it mattered

It made the camera something people carried every day, decades before phones. Tens of millions of 110 cameras were sold, and even an SLR was made for it.

## Limits and what replaced it

Small negatives meant visible [[g:grain|grain]] in prints larger than snapshot size, and the film could not lie perfectly flat. Compact 35mm cameras with autofocus replaced 110 in the 1980s.
