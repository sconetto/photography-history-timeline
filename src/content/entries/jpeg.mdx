## How it began

Digital pictures were huge for 1980s disks and modems. The Joint Photographic Experts Group, set up by standards bodies, worked from 1986 on a common way to compress photos. The standard was approved in 1992.

## How it works

The image is split into 8×8 pixel blocks. Each block is turned into a set of frequencies, and fine detail and colour, which the eye notices least, are stored with less precision or dropped. The rest is packed tightly.

## Why it mattered

Pictures shrank enough to fit on memory cards and travel over the internet. Digital cameras, the web, email and phones all rely on it.

## Limits and what replaced it

Each save loses some detail, and heavy compression shows blocks. Photographers who want everything use [[raw-dng|raw files]].
