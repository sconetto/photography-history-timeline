## How it began

Arthur Korn sent photographs over telegraph wires in Germany in 1907, and AT&T offered a service in the 1920s. On 1 January 1935 the Associated Press opened a network linking newspapers across the United States.

## How it works

The print is wrapped around a rotating drum and scanned line by line by a light and a photocell. Brightness becomes a varying electric signal sent down a phone line. At the other end a light, varied by the signal, exposes photographic paper on a drum turning in step.

## Why it mattered

The first picture sent on the AP network showed a plane crash in the Adirondacks. Newspapers could print pictures of distant events the same day, and agencies built their business on it.

## Limits and what replaced it

Quality was modest and each picture took minutes. Digital cameras such as the [[kodak-dcs|Kodak DCS]] and the internet replaced it.
