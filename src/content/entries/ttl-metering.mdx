## How it began

External meters did not know which lens or filter was in front of the film. In 1963 the Japanese Topcon RE Super placed a cadmium-sulphide cell in the SLR’s mirror. The Pentax Spotmatic followed in 1964 and made the idea popular.

## How it works

Light passing through the lens reaches a cell inside the camera, through the mirror or near the viewfinder prism. The reading accounts for the lens’s [[g:focal-length|focal length]], filters, extension tubes and close focusing.

## Why it mattered

It made metering accurate in difficult cases such as close-ups and long lenses, and made automatic exposure on SLRs practical.

## Limits and what replaced it

A single reading still averages the scene. Multi-zone metering, first in Nikon’s FA of 1983, compares areas against stored patterns; digital cameras meter directly from the sensor.
