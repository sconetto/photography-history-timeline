## How it began

[[flash-powder|Flash powder]] was dangerous, smoky and hard to time. In 1929 Johannes Ostermeier in Germany patented a bulb filled with aluminium foil and oxygen, sold as the Vacublitz; the Sashalite followed in the US.

## How it works

A small battery current heats a filament coated with a primer, which ignites the metal inside. It burns in the oxygen in about 1/50 s, giving a bright flash, all within the sealed glass. A flash gun fires the bulb in step with the shutter.

## Why it mattered

It was safe enough to use indoors among crowds, and it could be synchronised with the shutter. Press photographers with [[speed-graphic|Speed Graphics]] and flashbulbs defined the look of news pictures from the 1930s to the 1950s.

## Limits and what replaced it

Each bulb worked once, was hot after firing, and cost money. [[electronic-flash|Electronic flash]] replaced it from the 1950s and 1960s, though cheap flashcubes lasted into the 1980s.
