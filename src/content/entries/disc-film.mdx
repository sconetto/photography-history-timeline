## How it began

In 1982 Kodak launched Disc cameras: thin, automatic cameras with motor wind and built-in flash, loaded with a disc of film. It sold 25 million cameras in the first few years.

## How it works

Fifteen frames are arranged around a flat disc that rotates after each shot. Kodak used a fast, high-quality glass aspheric lens and a new fine-grain film to get the most from the tiny frame.

## Why it mattered

The cameras were simple and reliable, and Disc pushed the industry towards easy, automatic snapshot cameras. Its new film emulsion technology was later used in other films.

## Limits and what replaced it

The 8×10 mm frame was smaller than [[film-110|110]], and prints from photo labs were often grainy and soft. Autofocus 35mm compacts replaced it, and Kodak stopped making the film in 1999.
