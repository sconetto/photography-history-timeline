## How it began

Scholars such as Ibn al-Haytham and Roger Bacon had written about how curved glass magnifies. Around 1286 someone in northern Italy, probably near Pisa, made convex lenses to set in a frame and hold before the eyes. In 1306 the friar Giordano da Pisa told a Florence congregation that the art of making spectacles was not yet twenty years old.

## How it works

A convex lens is thicker in the middle than at the edges. Light passing through it bends towards the thick part, so rays from a near page converge as if they came from further away, which an ageing eye can focus.

## Why it mattered

Spectacles created a trade in grinding and polishing glass lenses. Within three centuries, lenses that were good and cheap enough were available to put in the opening of a [[camera-obscura]], and later to build telescopes and microscopes.

## Limits and what replaced it

Early lenses were small, weak and full of bubbles and colour fringes. Better glass, calculation and the achromatic lens slowly turned the craft into a science.
