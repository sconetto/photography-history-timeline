## How it began

In 1991 the musician Graham Nash and printer Mac Holbert began printing photographs on an Iris printer, made for proofing. Their studio, Nash Editions, showed that inkjet could make fine prints. Pigment ink printers from Epson and others followed around 2000.

## How it works

The print head fires millions of tiny drops of ink in several colours, placed in patterns so fine that the eye blends them. Pigment inks resist fading far better than early dye inks.

## Why it mattered

Colour printing had needed a professional lab. With [[photoshop|Photoshop]] and an inkjet, photographers controlled the whole process at home, and museums began to collect and show inkjet prints.

## Limits and what replaced it

Early prints faded quickly, and the medium was looked down on at first. Better inks and papers changed that; it remains the main fine-art printing method.
