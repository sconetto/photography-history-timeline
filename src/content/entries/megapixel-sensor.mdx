## How it began

Early [[ccd|CCDs]] had a few thousand to a few hundred thousand pixels. In 1986 Kodak’s research labs made a sensor with 1.4 million pixels.

## How it works

The sensor was a grid of light-sensitive cells, each building up an electric charge in proportion to the light, read out one by one. More, smaller cells give more detail.

## Why it mattered

A million pixels is about what a small photographic print needs, so the sensor showed that digital pictures could rival film for everyday use. Kodak built it into its first professional digital cameras.

## Limits and what replaced it

Smaller pixels gather less light, adding [[g:noise|noise]]. Better sensor design, such as [[cmos-sensor|CMOS]] and backside illumination, made high pixel counts practical.
