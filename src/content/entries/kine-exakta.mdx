## How it began

Ihagee in Dresden made a reflex camera for 127 roll film, the Exakta, in 1933. In 1936 its designer Karl Nüchterlein adapted it for 35mm cine film: the Kine Exakta, one of the first 35mm single-lens reflexes. A Soviet camera, the Sport, has a claim to have come slightly earlier.

## How it works

A mirror behind the lens reflects the image up to a ground glass, as in [[zahn-reflex|Zahn’s box]]. When the shutter is released the mirror flips up and the [[g:focal-plane-shutter|focal-plane shutter]] exposes the film. What you see is what the lens sees.

## Why it mattered

Rangefinder cameras such as the [[leica-i|Leica]] could not show exactly what long or close-up lenses would capture. Through-the-lens viewing solved that, and made the 35mm SLR useful for science, nature and close-up work.

## Limits and what replaced it

The image on the screen was reversed left to right, and the mirror stayed up after each shot. The [[pentaprism]] and the [[instant-return-mirror|instant-return mirror]] fixed both.
