## How it began

Folmer & Schwing in New York made large reflex cameras called Graflex. In 1912 they launched the Speed Graphic, a lighter folding camera with a [[g:focal-plane-shutter|focal-plane shutter]] for action, and it became the tool of newspaper photographers.

## How it works

The front folds out on a bed, and bellows connect the lens to the back, which takes 4×5 inch sheet film in holders. A rangefinder and a wire-frame finder let photographers focus and aim quickly; a [[flashbulb]] gun fired in step with the shutter.

## Why it mattered

The big negative printed sharply on rough newsprint, and the camera could take a beating. From the 1920s to the 1950s most famous American news pictures were made with one, including Joe Rosenthal’s Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima in 1945.

## Limits and what replaced it

Each sheet had to be changed by hand, so a photographer might carry a dozen shots. 35mm cameras such as the [[nikon-f|Nikon F]] replaced it in the 1960s.
