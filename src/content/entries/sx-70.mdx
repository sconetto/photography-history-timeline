## How it began

Polaroid’s early [[instant-photography|instant films]] had to be peeled apart, leaving messy chemicals. Edwin Land wanted a picture that came out complete. After years of work, the SX-70 appeared in 1972.

## How it works

The camera folds flat. Opened, it is a reflex: mirrors fold the light path so the image is seen through the lens. After the exposure, rollers push the picture out and spread a reagent between its layers. An opaque layer protects the image while it develops in daylight.

## Why it mattered

Pictures developed in your hand in minutes, with no waste. It was an engineering landmark, and artists were drawn to its look and to manipulating the soft emulsion.

## Limits and what replaced it

Film was expensive, and colours shifted with temperature. Digital cameras ended most instant photography by the 2000s, but SX-70 film was revived and is made today.
