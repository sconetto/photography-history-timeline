## How it began

In 1819 the scientist John Herschel reported that "hyposulphite of soda", now called sodium thiosulfate, dissolves silver salts. Twenty years later, hearing of Daguerre's and Talbot's work, he made his own photographs within days. He fixed them with hypo, and told Talbot and others.

## How it works

After exposure and development, a photograph still contains silver salts that light has not touched. In daylight they would slowly darken and swallow the image. A [[g:fixer|fixer]] turns these insoluble salts into soluble compounds that wash away, leaving only the image silver. Talbot had been using strong salt, which only partly stabilised the image; hypo removes the unexposed salts properly.

## Why it mattered

Photographs became permanent. Daguerre adopted hypo too, and it has fixed nearly every silver photograph since. Herschel also coined the vocabulary: in 1839 he wrote of "photography", and soon of "negative" and "positive" images. Later he used "snapshot".

## Limits and what replaced it

Prints that are not washed well enough to remove leftover hypo yellow and fade over decades. Faster ammonium thiosulfate "rapid fixers" came in the 20th century; the principle is unchanged.
