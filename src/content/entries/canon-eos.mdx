## How it began

Minolta’s Maxxum 7000 of 1985 brought [[autofocus]] to SLRs. Canon answered in 1987 by starting again: the EOS 650 and a new mount, EF, that did not fit its old lenses.

## How it works

There are no mechanical links between body and lens, only electrical contacts. Each lens has its own focusing motor, later the fast and quiet ultrasonic USM type, and its own electronic [[g:aperture|aperture]].

## Why it mattered

Putting the motor in the lens made autofocus fast and allowed big telephoto lenses to focus quickly. It was a gamble that angered owners of old lenses, but it paid off: Canon became the leading maker for sport, news and, later, digital SLRs.

## Limits and what replaced it

The SLR mirror and the long flange distance limited lens design. Canon replaced EF with the RF mirrorless mount in 2018.
