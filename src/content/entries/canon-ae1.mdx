## How it began

By the mid-1970s Japanese SLRs were good but complicated and expensive. Canon designed the AE-1 to be built cheaply in large numbers, with electronic modules and plastic parts, and advertised it heavily, including on television.

## How it works

The photographer picks a shutter speed; a built-in light meter measures through the lens, and a microprocessor sets the aperture automatically. A battery powers the shutter and electronics. It was one of the first cameras with a central processor managing its functions.

## Why it mattered

The AE-1 sold in the millions and brought automatic SLR photography to amateurs. It showed that electronics, not precision mechanics, would drive cameras forward, which led to autofocus and eventually to digital cameras.

## Limits and what replaced it

Without a battery it does not work, and it has no manual-exposure aperture priority or autofocus. Canon's fully electronic EOS system (1987) succeeded it.
