## How it began

Johann Heinrich Schulze had shown that light, not heat, darkens [[silver-salts|silver salts]]. The Swedish chemist Carl Wilhelm Scheele took the work further and published his results in 1777, in a book mostly about air and fire.

## How it works

Scheele spread silver chloride on paper and placed it in a spectrum cast by a prism. It blackened fastest in the violet and hardly at all in the red. He also showed that the black deposit was metallic silver: ammonia dissolved the unchanged chloride but left the blackened part behind, a first hint at how a picture might be fixed. See [[p:silver|silver halide chemistry]].

## Why it mattered

He explained what the dark trace actually was, and showed that the colour of the light matters. That is why early plates were blind to red and why darkrooms could use red light safely.

## Limits and what replaced it

Scheele was not trying to make pictures. Turning his chemistry into images, and fixing them, took another sixty years.
