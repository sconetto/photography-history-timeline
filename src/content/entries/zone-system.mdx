## How it began

Teaching at the Art Center School in Los Angeles around 1939–40, Ansel Adams and Fred Archer turned their working methods into a system that students could learn.

## How it works

Tones are divided into eleven zones, from pure black (0) to pure white (X), each one [[g:stop|stop]] apart. A [[light-meter|light meter]] reading gives middle grey, Zone V. The photographer decides where a key shadow should fall and exposes for it, then lengthens or shortens [[g:developer|development]] to move the highlights up or down.

## Why it mattered

It gave photographers precise control of contrast, at the time of exposure, and a shared language for talking about tones. Adams’s landscapes made it famous.

## Limits and what replaced it

It suits sheet film, where each negative can be developed separately. With roll film and digital, the same ideas survive as exposing for the highlights and reading a histogram.
