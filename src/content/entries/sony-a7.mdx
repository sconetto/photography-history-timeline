## How it began

[[g:mirrorless|Mirrorless]] cameras dropped the reflex mirror of the [[g:slr|SLR]] and showed the sensor's live image on a screen or electronic viewfinder. Panasonic's Lumix G1 (2008) was the first with interchangeable lenses, using a smaller Micro Four Thirds sensor. In 2013 Sony put a [[full-frame-digital|full-frame sensor]] in a mirrorless body: the α7.

## How it works

Without a mirror box, the lens mount sits close to the sensor, so the body can be small and the lenses compact. The sensor runs all the time, feeding the electronic viewfinder, autofocus and exposure systems directly, with a preview of the final exposure.

## Why it mattered

The α7 showed that full-frame quality need not come in a big SLR. Sony's market share grew, and in 2018 Canon and Nikon launched full-frame mirrorless systems, ending the long reign of the reflex mirror that began with the [[nikon-f|Nikon F]].

## Limits and what replaced it

Early models had short battery life and laggy viewfinders, and the lens range was small at first. Later generations improved all of these, and stacked sensors brought very fast readout.
