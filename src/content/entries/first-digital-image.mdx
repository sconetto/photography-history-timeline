## How it began

At the US National Bureau of Standards in 1957, Russell Kirsch and colleagues built a drum scanner for the SEAC computer. The first picture they scanned was a photo of his three-month-old son Walden.

## How it works

The scanner measured light reflected from small areas of the photograph, one at a time, and stored each as a number: black or white. Together they made a square grid of 176×176 [[g:megapixel|pixels]].

## Why it mattered

It showed that a picture could become data that a computer could store, change and analyse. Image processing, satellite imaging and digital photography all build on this.

## Limits and what replaced it

It was a scan of an existing photo, not a camera. Electronic sensors such as the [[ccd|CCD]] made digital capture possible.
