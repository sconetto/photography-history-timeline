## How it began

Each air-to-glass surface reflects about 4% of the light, so a lens with eight surfaces loses about a third of it, and the stray reflections fog the image as flare. In 1935 Alexander Smakula at Zeiss patented a way to coat lenses with a thin evaporated layer. The German military kept it secret for some years.

## How it works

Light reflected from the top of the coating and from the glass beneath it travel distances that differ by half a wavelength, so the two reflections cancel. Magnesium fluoride has the right refractive index to make that cancellation nearly complete for green light.

## Why it mattered

Coating made complex lenses practical: more elements meant better correction without a haze of flare. It also improved contrast in colour photography, which was just beginning. Coated lenses became normal after the Second World War.

## Limits and what replaced it

One layer works best for one colour. Multi-layer coatings from the 1970s and nanostructured coatings in the 2000s reduced reflections across the whole spectrum.
