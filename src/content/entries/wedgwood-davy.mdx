## How it began

Thomas Wedgwood grew up among artists and scientists and knew of the [[camera-obscura]]. In the 1790s he tried to capture its image on paper or leather treated with silver nitrate. In 1802 his friend Humphry Davy published their results.

## How it works

Objects laid on the sensitised paper in sunlight left white shapes where they blocked the light, a photogram. Paintings on glass could be copied the same way. The images in the camera obscura were too faint to register in the time they had.

## Why it mattered

It was the first published attempt to make pictures with light and chemistry, and it named the central problem. The pictures could only be looked at by candlelight, because daylight kept darkening the unexposed silver until the whole sheet went black.

## Limits and what replaced it

Without a fixer the images were lost. [[heliography|Niépce]] and [[daguerreotype|Daguerre]] succeeded with other materials; the fix for silver images, [[hypo-fixer|hypo]], came in 1839.
