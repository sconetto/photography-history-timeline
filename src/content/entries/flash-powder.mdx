## How it began

Burning magnesium ribbon had been used as a light since the 1860s, but it burned slowly. In 1887 Adolf Miethe and Johannes Gaedicke mixed fine magnesium powder with an oxidiser, so the whole charge went off at once.

## How it works

The powder burns in a tiny fraction of a second and gives off intense light, rich in the blue that the [[g:emulsion|emulsions]] of the day were most sensitive to. Combined with fast [[gelatin-dry-plate|dry plates]], that was enough to record a scene that daylight never reached.

## Why it mattered

It opened up the dark: interiors, night scenes and the slums that reformers such as Jacob Riis photographed to change public opinion.

## Limits and what replaced it

It was loud, smoky and dangerous, and burned photographers badly. The enclosed flashbulb (1929) and then electronic flash replaced it.
